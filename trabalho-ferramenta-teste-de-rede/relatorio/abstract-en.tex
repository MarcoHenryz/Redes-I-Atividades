\begin{center}
  \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\vspace{\baselineskip}

\noindent This work evaluates the performance of network data transmission between two computers, comparing the TCP and UDP transport protocols over two distinct
physical media: a wireless network (WiFi) and a direct wired connection
(Ethernet). Using a custom testing tool, fixed-size packets (500 bytes) were
transmitted in both directions between the hosts, measuring throughput, in
packets per second and in Mbit/s, and the packet loss rate at the receiver.
The results highlight the superiority of the wired connection in both
throughput and reliability, as well as the impact of UDP's lack of flow
control, which is responsible for high losses over the wireless medium.

\vfill

\noindent\textbf{Key words:} TCP. UDP. Throughput. Packet Loss. Computer networks.
